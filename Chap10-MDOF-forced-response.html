1 · One building, n independent oscillators Paz 8.1

Each mode is its own oscillator, in its own hue. The oscillators do not talk to each other; each only hears φjT{F}, the work of the load through its mode shape. Bars: φjT{F}, one per mode; panel 2 has the modal parts and their sum.

2 · The peaks do not coincide Paz 8.6; 2024 L23

Max of a sum is not the sum of the maxes. SRSS is an estimate, not a bound: with ξ = 5%, Ex. 8.1 has it 9% low at floor 1 and 10% high at floor 2. In Ex. 8.2 mode 2 holds 1.3% of the mass, so all the rules agree. Peaks over a window of 6T₁.

4 · ω₂/ω₁ toward 1 Paz 8.6; Chopra 13.7; L23 p. 61

Two oscillators under one ground motion, each scaled to a unit peak, so that only the timing is in play. As r → 1 they fall into step: the sum grows toward ‖m‖₁ for like signs and shrinks to nothing for opposite signs, while SRSS sits still. "Closely spaced" depends on damping.

6 · The building shakes, mode by mode Paz 8.2; Chopra 13.2; L24, L29

Ground motion drives every mode with the same ÿs; only Γj decides how much each one takes. Effective masses add up to the total. The spectrum is SA/Ag, the pseudo-acceleration over the peak ground acceleration; MjeffSA(Tj) is mode j's peak base shear. All the buildings are on one scale; grey ghost: the exact response. The record is Paz's Table 5.3, not Chopra's, so peaks differ from the handout.

7 · How many modes? not in Paz

Error of the exact history truncated to N modes. Effective mass speaks for base shear. Local and acceleration quantities want more modes, and the error need not shrink steadily.

8 · Combine last not in Paz; 2024 L24

Floor peaks have lost their signs, so their difference is no drift at any time. Combine the modal shears, not the floor peaks. "One mode only": nothing to combine, every floor's peak exact, and combine first is still wrong.

9 · Frequency sweep Paz 8.4, 8.5

A resonance at each mode, and at each resonance the building takes that mode's shape. Peaks shrink with ωj² and the shape is purest at the lowest modes. Below ω₁ the floors follow the load; just above it they swing against it. Between the peaks the loaded floor can stand still (a dip). Tap a floor to load it.
