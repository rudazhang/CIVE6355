Which pair? Chopra 11.4; the notes' question

The first mode has to be a target. After that, the choice of the second barely moves the response. Leave mode 1 out and you over-damp most of the mass, unconservatively. Cell color: worst |ξi − ξ|/ξ over the modes holding 90% of the mass. Tap a cell or row to load it above.

Three constructions on one building Paz 20.24, 20.25; Chopra 11.4

A construction is a choice of what happens in the modes you did not specify, and of the zero pattern. At 2 DOF all three are one matrix, since both ξ are specified. At 3 DOF they part. The entries are not dashpots: the modal sum couples floors that share no story. Rayleigh's sparsity is why nonlinear analysis uses it. Paz prints 573.5, −199.3, 313.9 for Ex. 7.1: rounded a₁, a₂.

Who dies first Paz 20.2; Chopra 11.4

Each model has its own decay pattern; all share one ξ₁, so mode 1 never changes (Rayleigh: while mode 1 is a target). Mass-proportional: one decay rate, the whole building fades together. Equal ξ: the same number of cycles, so the fast modes go first in seconds; this is what measurements suggest. Modal sum of too few modes: the rest ring on.

The oscillators start talking Chopra 11.5; handout Problem B

Dampers do not change [M] and [K], so they do not change the modes; they add Aij = Σ csδsiδsj. Equal dampers stay diagonal only on a building with equal ks: cs ∝ ks gives β{φ}iT[K]{φ}j = 0. Off the diagonal, modes drive each other. A damper belongs where the first mode's drift is largest.

[A] = [Φ]T[C][Φ]; ξi = Aii/(2ωi)

More damper is not more damping Chopra 11.5; handout B(e)

True ξ₁ peaks, then falls, because the damper locks the story and a locked story dissipates nothing. The diagonal of [A] climbs forever. At a modest damper decoupling is fine and engineers do it; past the peak it reports a building that barely moves, while the true drift has only moved upstairs. True ξ₁ is read from the decay.

The hinge that will not yield Chopra 20.7; handout Problem C

Nothing physical has changed but the damping model, and RI hides the nonlinearity. a₁[K] with initial stiffness puts a huge dashpot beside the stiff hinge; it carries the force the hinge could not. An engineer reading RI would report a hinge with no demand and too large an upper-story drift. Rayleigh 5%, targets 1, 2. A stiffer hinge makes it worse.
